Point Hub

Our own scoreboard. 1.67 times the size of the Tech4Sport box, a small score panel facing each bench, two USB-C ports and nothing else. Seventeen steps over a weekend.

A

Case

Plywood, glue, paint. About 5 hours plus a day to dry.

about 5 h + a day to dry
01

Cut the parts

Seven rectangles from one 1250 × 1250 sheet of 6 mm poplar plywood. The store saw at Jumbo or Hornbach does these straight cuts.

  • iPoplar keeps the box light. Birch works too and adds about 1.5 kg.
02

Cut windows and holes

Drill a hole in each window corner and join them with the jigsaw. Round holes with a hole saw or wood bit.

  • ✓Masking tape on the cut line, good side down: no splinters.
  • iCheck the USB-C cut-out against the sockets that actually arrive.
03

Glue the shell

Glue and screw the bottom, both ends, the top and the front frame to the battens. The back panel B stays loose: it is screwed on last. Glue a 15 mm block into each corner of the back edge and one in the middle of the top and the bottom, flush with the edge: the back panel screws into them.

  • iPre-drill Ø2.5 for every screw near an edge.
04

Fit the bottom

Turn the box over. Glue the backing plate Y inside, then fit the stand cup, the two threaded inserts and the four feet.

05

Paint

Round every outside edge with 120-grit sandpaper, about 3 mm. Then two coats of matte black, inside too, so nothing reflects behind the LEDs. The front face alone gets gloss black: behind the smoked sheet, the screen and its border read as one black surface when the board is off.

  • iSpray outdoors, or roll water-based paint indoors. Fill the plywood grain on the front face first, or it shows through the gloss.
B

Panels

The box lies face-down from here until the electronics.

about 2 h
06

Front sheet in

Lay the box face-down on a blanket. Peel the film off the inner face of the smoked polycarbonate and drop it into the front frame. Same for the two small end sheets.

  • iKeep the outer film on until the very end: it protects against scratches.
  • iSmoked sheet: run the brightness about a fifth higher than behind clear. The contrast in a bright hall is much better.
07

Six modules

Lay the modules on the sheet, LEDs down, all arrows pointing the same way. Connect the short ribbons 1 → 2 → 3 and 4 → 5 → 6.

  • ✓Leave the IN of modules 1 and 4 free: the driver plugs in there.
  • iBefore ordering, get a photo of the driver chips: plain chips like ICN2037 or FM6124 work, “S-PWM” chips like ICN2153 do not.
08

Bars across

Lay three steel bars across the modules and screw them into the two long battens. The modules’ own magnets snap onto the bars: no screws, so a module swaps out in seconds.

  • iIf your modules come without magnets, order M3 magnet standoffs from the same seller.
09

Bench panels

One module behind each end window, arrows up. Twist four turn buttons over its edges.

  • iStand the box upright again after this step.
C

Electronics

Low voltage only: nothing in here can give you a shock.

about 4 h
10

Lay out the electronics

On the bottom panel behind the modules. Converter and fuse box sit on the aluminium plate. The DATA socket goes through a small USB-C hub to the Pi’s network port; the charging module feeds the hub, so the tablet charges over the same cable. Fit the two USB-C sockets in the back panel now; their cables reach inside. Put the Pi close to the back panel: its built-in Wi-Fi is the board’s only link to the internet.

  • iLeave 15 cm of slack on every cable so the back panel can come off.
  • iOne 10 kΩ resistor keeps the panels dark while the Pi boots, so a weak charger can’t trip. The spot is marked on the wiring card.
11

Power wiring

POWER socket → USB-C board → 5 A fuse → converter (1 mm²) → fuse box (2.5 mm²). A ferrule on every wire end. The small TVS diode goes across the POWER socket’s + and −.

  • iConnect the converter’s −Vin to −V: the USB-C board talks to the Pi and needs a shared ground.
12

First power-up

Nothing else connected. Plug a small phone charger into POWER and measure at the fuse box.

  • iA weak charger is the safety net: it cannot push enough current to damage anything.
13

Connect everything

Every panel and the Pi gets its own 5 A fuse. Ribbon OUT 1 to module 1, OUT 2 to module 4, OUT 3 to the left bench panel and on to the right one. The USB-C board, clock and temperature sensor share the I²C wires to pins 27 and 28 of the tall header.

  • iPanel power leads that don’t reach: extend with 1.5 mm².
14

Test

With the back still open, slide in the SD card we prepared. Power up with a laptop charger: a test pattern runs on all eight modules. Leave it for 30 minutes.

D

Finish

Ready for the hall.

about 1 h
15

Close up

Put the back panel on and drive in the six T20 screws. Handles on top, a black rubber cap on each front corner, and the plug in the horn hole. The back corners stay bare, so the back panel lifts straight off. The POINT HUB lettering goes on the bottom-right of the front border, the label beside the ports.

  • iThe horn hole stays plugged: horn wiring is ready inside if you ever want one.
16

Set it up

On the table, on a speaker stand, or on a tripod or light stand. On the table, loop a strap through each handle and around the back edge of the table. Charger into POWER, tablet into DATA: one cable carries the scores and charges the tablet, and the scorer PIN isn’t needed on it. The board joins the hall’s Wi-Fi for live scores, but has no Wi-Fi of its own: a laptop for setup or OpenVolley takes the same cable.

  • ✓Any USB-C charger works from 18 W; 65 W or more gives full brightness and charges the tablet.
  • iOptional: fold out the two legs under the front edge. They lift the front 5.5 cm, so the screen tilts 15° up toward the first referee on the stand. It then leans on its back feet: keep the strap on. Switch on “Legs out” in the console, and the bench screens are drawn turned back 15°, so they stay level. Raising it on a stand helps more against people in the way.
  • ✕Never plug the DATA cable into the school’s network: it’s for the tablet or a laptop only.
  • iOn a stand, put a sandbag on the feet instead of the strap.
  • ✕Never in direct sun or by a sunny window: the box would overheat. It dims itself from 50 °C.
  • iKeep a flip scoreboard with digits of 12 cm or more in the kit: Swiss Volley requires one if the board fails.
  • iComing in from a cold car: wait 45 minutes before switching on, or condensation can short the modules.
17

Open it

Unplug the charger. Undo the six T20 screws and lift the back panel off: everything inside is in reach. The back panel carries the fuse map, four spare fuses and a spare SD card.

  • ✓Swapping the SD card or a fuse takes under a minute.
  • iThe back takes a T20 key on purpose: in a public hall, anything that opens by hand gets opened. Keep the key in the kit bag.
  • iThe two USB-C cables can stay plugged in: set the back panel down next to the box.
