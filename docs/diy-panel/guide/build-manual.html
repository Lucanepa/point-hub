Point Hub

Our own scoreboard. 1.67 times the size of the Tech4Sport box, a small score panel facing each bench, a front that tilts toward the first referee, a touch screen for the scorer in a flap at the back, two USB-C ports. 27 steps.

A

Workshop

Saw, hole saw and metal work at the community-centre open workshop (GZ Hirzenbach: wood and metal, Tue 14–21, Sat 11–17; own tools and material allowed).

one workshop afternoon
01

Get the parts

The plywood comes from Jumbo’s online cut-to-size service: Oecoplan poplar, 6 mm, every piece already a rectangle. Jumbo cuts nothing under 230 × 150, so the bottom cap K, the cheeks H, the backing plates Y and the pocket walls come as oversize blanks you cut yourself. Add five beech battens 15 × 20 × 1000.

  • iMeasure the real thickness at pickup: “6 mm” can be anything from 5.6 to 6.3. The outer joints get sanded flush later.
  • iPencil each part’s letter on it as it comes off the saw.
02

Cut the openings

With the jigsaw: the front window in A, a window in each end D, the flap opening in B and the two handle cut-outs in the top C1. Drill Ø10 in every corner first, then saw from hole to hole. Hole saws for the horn hole (Ø32). Ports, vents and the six back-screw holes in B. Leave the bottom C2’s holes until after the dry fit.

  • ✓Painter’s tape on the cut line and the good face down: the T101B blade cuts on the up stroke, so the face down stays clean.
  • iCheck the USB-C cut-outs against the sockets you actually have.
03

Rip the blanks

Cheeks H: rip the blank to 130, then saw corner to corner for two triangles. Backing plates Y: two pieces 140 × 208. Pocket walls: four strips 25 wide, two of them cut to 205. Leave K at 150 until the dry fit decides its depth.

  • iA batten clamped as a fence keeps long rips straight. Cut beside the line on the offcut side, then sand to it.
04

Make the metal parts

Two ratchet stays from the 25 × 4 aluminium flat, 200 long: a Ø4.5 pivot hole 12 mm from one end, then five notches 44, 75, 106, 137, 167 mm from it (0, 5, 10, 15 and 20°), 9 mm up from the lower edge. For each notch drill Ø6.5, then cut in from the edge with the jigsaw (T118B) on both sides of the hole and file the slot smooth. Cut three steel bars of 360 from the 20 × 4 flat, and the piano hinge to 1000 (T118A, or sandwich it in plywood and use T118B).

  • iThe bars are 360, not 368: the front frame they sit on is only 366 tall. Three of them need two 1 m bars.
  • iTape both stays together and drill through both: the notches then match.
B

Case

Back home: gluing, fitting and painting. Ventilate when painting; keep sawdust away from where you will wire later.

about 8 h + paint cure
05

Dry fit

Tape the box together without glue: bottom, both ends, top and back. Hold the front frame A in the front opening (tape along its top edge works as a hinge for now) and swing it: it needs 2 mm each side and 1 mm top and bottom. Then hold the K blank against A’s bottom edge and swing to 0° and to 20°.

  • ?Open point. The bottom cap K and the backing plates Y both want the middle of the floor: K slides over the front 11 cm, Y sits under the stand cup. Decide here, before gluing Y: (1) Y towards the back, with K riding over it on two thin runners, or (2) move Y and the stand cup off K’s path. Check K’s sweep at 0° and at 20°.
  • ?Open point. At 20° the bottom of A stands 126 mm out. A 104 mm cap reaches the floor only up to about 17°; at 20° it ends 18 mm short of the front edge. Mark K’s final depth from the 150 blank here, knowing a deeper K needs more floor.
06

Glue the shell

Glue (D3, water-resistant) and screw the bottom C2, both ends D and the top C1. Battens go in with their 20 mm face on the panel and 15 mm into the box: one under the top along its front edge (the piano hinge screws into it), one on the bottom along the back edge (the back panel screws into it). Blocks from offcuts at the top back corners and middle, and in the inside corners where the ends meet the top and bottom, kept 40 mm back from the front so the frame, cheeks and stays clear them. Painter’s tape every 15 cm holds the long joints; clamps where they reach.

  • iPre-drill Ø2.5 for every 3.5 mm screw: beech is hard.
  • iThe glue stays open about 10 minutes: one joint at a time.
  • ✓Before it sets: the front opening is 1008 × 368 and square (equal diagonals).
07

Stand cup and threads

Drill the bottom where the dry fit put Y: Ø45 for the stand cup, and holes for the two thread inserts (the size the inserts ask for). Glue the two Y layers together and inside the bottom, then fit the 35 mm stand flange with four M4 × 16 and screw the ¼″-20 and ⅜″-16 inserts in from below.

  • iThe 6 mm bottom alone is too thin for bolts and inserts: they need the 12 mm plate.
08

Screen pocket and flap

Glue the pocket walls P2 and P3 round the flap opening on the inside of B, and the pocket back P1 on top: a box 25 mm deep. Glue the two flap layers F together (12 mm) and trim them to the real screen, 10 mm clear of the walls all round. Two torque hinges along the flap’s top edge, into a block under the pocket’s top wall; a magnetic catch on a block at the bottom. A slot through F and P1 for the display ribbon.

  • iThe torque hinges hold the flap at any angle; it swings out like an awning from closed to about 75° open. Fit a stop there, for example a short cord from the flap to the pocket.
  • iCatches screw into blocks or battens, never into 6 mm plywood; pre-drill 2 mm. No counter-plate: a steel washer.
09

Hang the front frame

Lay A face-down. Screw one leaf of the piano hinge along its top border, knuckle at A’s top back edge so nothing shows from the front. Offer A into the opening with a 0.5 mm card under it (1.5 mm stays free under the top, which the 3 mm round on A’s top front edge needs at 20°) and 2 mm on each side, and screw the other leaf into the batten under the top. It comes off again for painting: the screw holes are set now.

  • iPre-drill 2 mm for the hinge screws.
10

Cap, cheeks and stays

Glue each cheek H to a strip of batten on the back of A’s end border, just inboard of the bench screen. Hang K from A’s bottom inside edge so its back edge slides on the floor. For the stays: a block on A’s back at each bottom corner carries the pivot (a 3.5 × 30 screw with a washer, not tight); through each end wall’s bottom border, 60 mm behind the front and 12 mm above the floor, an M6 × 30 bolt through a 12 mm block with a nylon lock nut is the pin. Lift A’s bottom, drop a notch on the pin, and check 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20° with the speed square.

  • iK hangs from A’s bottom edge on three 50 mm butt hinges (3 × 12 screws; glue a 6 mm offcut doubler under each leaf on K and on A first, or the screws come through). Cut K’s depth at the dry fit (about 140 from the 150 blank): at 20° a 104 mm cap no longer reaches the floor.
  • ?Open point. The stay, its pin and the cheek share the 22 mm strip at each end of A. If the cheek touches the pin’s nut, trim the cheek’s bottom back corner: K closes that part of the gap anyway.
  • ✓Teeth don’t creep when a ball hits the front.
11

Handles

Drop each K16 handle into its cut-out from above, the rim flat on the top. Six M4 × 16 pan-head Torx screws through the rim, a lock nut on each inside.

  • iThe tray sits 7.6 mm deep under the top: check it clears the blocks and the pocket.
12

Pull-out foot

Two batten rails, 205 long, glued under the bottom from front to back, 602 apart inside. A strip of plywood offcut screwed under each overhangs 12 mm inward: the foot T rides on these lips like a drawer. Pulled out 12 cm it turns the 22 cm base into 34 cm, so a ball can’t tip the box over backwards. Two screws in a slot stop it at full travel; a turn button on the back end of a rail holds it in.

  • iWith the foot in, check that a tripod plate still reaches the threads; if not, cut a notch in T’s front edge.
13

Paint

Take the hinge, stays, handles and catches off again and label them. Round every outside edge with sandpaper, about 3 mm (P120, then P180), and fill the grain on A’s front face. Two coats of matte black everywhere, inside too, so nothing reflects behind the LEDs. A’s front face gets gloss black: behind the smoked sheet, screen and border read as one black surface when the board is off.

  • iKeep the 3 mm round on A’s top front edge: at 20° that corner swings up about 2 mm into the top.
  • iPaint at the workshop if they allow it, or on a balcony on a drop sheet. Optional: a 2K matte clear on top against splashes.
14

Pads and corner caps

When the paint has fully cured: put the hardware back, then four clear rubber pads under the rails and two under the foot’s back end. Black rubber caps on the box’s four front corners, trimmed so they cover only the ends’ 6 mm edge: the front frame must swing past them.

C

Screens

The front frame comes off its hinge once more for the modules.

about 2 h
15

Modules on the front frame

Unscrew the hinge from the batten (it stays on A) and lay A face-down on a blanket. Peel the inner film off the smoked sheet and lay it over the window. Six modules on top, LEDs down, all arrows the same way, short ribbons 1 → 2 → 3 and 4 → 5 → 6. Four M3 magnetic screws in each module. Glue blocks on A’s top and bottom border, packed to the magnet height, and screw the three steel bars onto them: the magnets snap on, so a module swaps out in seconds.

  • ✓Leave the IN of modules 1 and 4 free: the driver plugs in there.
  • iKeep the sheet’s outer film on until the very end.
16

Bench screens

One smoked sheet and one module behind each end window, arrows up. Twist four turn buttons over the module’s edges.

  • iThe stay’s pin block sits just under the bench screen: keep the bottom border free.
17

Hang it again

Screw the hinge back into the batten, same holes. Plug the ribbons and power leads with about 12 cm of slack, then tilt to 20° and back with everything plugged in: nothing may pull tight or catch on a cheek.

D

Electronics

Low voltage only: nothing in here can give you a shock. Solder at home, with a window open.

about 5 h
18

Lay out the electronics

On the floor, behind the strip K slides over. Pi 5 with its cooler and the bonnet on the left near the flap, the I²C parts and the horn switch beside it; converter and fuse box on the aluminium plate on the right, the USB-C board and the two current sensors near POWER, the tablet charger and the USB-C hub under the pocket. Fit the two USB-C sockets in the back panel; their cables reach inside.

  • iLeave 15 cm of slack on everything that goes to the back panel, so it can come off.
19

Power path

POWER socket → TVS diode across + and − → STUSB4500 → INA226 (0x41, A0 bridged) → 5 A fuse → converter, in 1 mm². Converter → INA226 (0x40, 2 mΩ shunt) → fuse box, in 2.5 mm². After the 5 A fuse, a 3 A fuse feeds the tablet charger into the USB-C hub’s charging input. A ferrule on every wire end.

  • iSet the STUSB4500 to “output only above 5 V”: a 5 V-only source leaves the box off instead of half-booting it.
  • iJoin the converter’s −Vin to −V: the USB-C board talks to the Pi and needs a shared ground.
  • iThe Pi takes its 5 V on the header pins from its own fuse; its USB-C socket stays empty.
20

First power-up

Nothing else connected. A small USB-C phone charger into POWER; measure at the fuse box.

  • iA weak charger is the safety net: it cannot push enough current to damage anything.
21

LED power and data

Every module, each bench module and the Pi on its own 5 A fuse (ways 1–9). Way 10 is the horn, 11 and 12 are spare. Ribbon OUT 1 to module 1, OUT 2 to module 4, OUT 3 to the left bench module and on to the right one.

  • iPanel power leads that don’t reach: extend with 1.5 mm².
22

I²C parts and the horn

The USB-C board, the clock, the temperature sensor, both current sensors and the MCP23017 share two wires to pins 27 and 28 (GPIO 0 and 1) of the tall header. MCP23017 pin GPA0 drives the isolated MOSFET module, which switches the buzzer’s 5 V from fuse way 10. The buzzer screws on from inside over the Ø32 hole. GPA1–3 drive the STATUS light and GPA4–6 the LINK light (red, green, blue through 150, 47 and 47 Ω); the POWER light hangs on 5 V through 330 Ω so it shows power even when the Pi is dead.

  • !Check before you rely on it. Chain 3 uses GPIO 2, 3 and 14, which is why I²C moved to pins 27/28. Confirm this still holds on the Pi 5 with the bonnet before you wire it.
  • iThe MCP23017 starts with every pin an input, so the horn stays off at boot.
23

Control screen

Screw the screen onto the flap’s outer face and pass its ribbon through the slot. The 300 mm DSI cable runs to the Pi 5’s display port with a loose loop at the hinge, so it flexes instead of folding.

  • !Check before you rely on it. Run all eight modules and the screen together. The LED driver needs one Pi core; if the modules flicker with the screen on, the screen stays out.
24

Test

With the back still off, slide in the prepared SD card and power up with a 65 W laptop charger. POWER lights green at once; STATUS blinks yellow while it starts, then turns green; LINK shows blue with the tablet on the cable, green when online (the full colour code is in BUILD.md §6 and on the back label). The power-on self-test runs, then a test pattern on all eight modules; leave it for 30 minutes. The diagnostics page shows what the self-test found (BUILD.md §6).

  • !Check before you rely on it. First light on the Pi 5 with Piomatter, the screen flicker test and the soak test are not done yet on this design. If the Pi 5 path fails, a Pi 4 2 GB with the hzeller library is the fallback (BUILD.md).
E

Finish

Ready for the hall.

about 1 h
25

Close up

Put the back panel on and drive the six T20 screws into the batten and the blocks; turn buttons hold the rest. Inside the back: the fuse map, spare fuses and a spare SD card. An EPDM D-seal round the back panel’s rim, a brush seal at the front frame’s gap, dust caps on the USB-C sockets. The POINT HUB lettering and the back label wait until after first light, when the port positions are final.

26

Set it up

On a table: lift the front frame’s bottom and drop the stays into the notch you want, 0 to 20° toward the first referee; swing the flap open for the scorer; pull the foot out. On a speaker stand or a tripod: foot in, flap as needed. Charger into POWER, tablet into DATA: one cable carries the scores and charges the tablet, and the scorer PIN isn’t needed on it.

  • ✓Any USB-C charger works from 18 W; 65 W or more gives full brightness and charges the tablet.
  • ✕Never plug the DATA cable into the school’s network: it’s for the tablet or a laptop only.
  • ✕Never in direct sun or by a sunny window: the box would overheat. It dims itself from 50 °C.
  • iKeep a flip scoreboard with digits of 12 cm or more in the kit: Swiss Volley requires one if the board fails.
  • iComing in from a cold car: wait 45 minutes before switching on, or condensation can short the modules.
27

Open it

Unplug the charger. Undo the six T20 screws, ease the back panel off and unplug the screen ribbon at the Pi: everything inside is in reach.

  • ✓Swapping the SD card or a fuse takes a minute.
  • iThe back takes a T20 key on purpose: in a public hall, anything that opens by hand gets opened. Keep the key in the kit bag.
